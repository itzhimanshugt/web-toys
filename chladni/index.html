Ernst Chladni, 1787

Ernst Florens Friedrich Chladni (1756–1827), a German physicist and musician, fixed thin brass and glass plates at one point, scattered sand over them and drew a violin bow along the edge. The sand gathered into figures along the lines that did not move. He published them in Entdeckungen über die Theorie des Klanges (Leipzig, 1787).

The square plate here uses the classic approximation u = cos(nπx) cos(mπy) ± cos(mπx) cos(nπy) with a nominal pitch of 22 Hz × (n² + m²). The disc uses Bessel modes Jm(kr) cos mθ with k at a zero of Jm′ (free rim), pitched by Chladni’s law f ∝ (m + 2n)², with m nodal diameters and n nodal circles. Both are idealised models; real plates shift with thickness, material and clamping.

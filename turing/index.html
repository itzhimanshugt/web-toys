∂u/∂t = Du∇²u − uv² + F(1 − u)
∂v/∂t = Dv∇²v + uv² − (F + k)v This dish uses Du = 1.0, Dv = 0.5, a 9-point Laplacian and a time step of 1. F feeds U in from outside; k removes V. The amber curve on the map is the saddle-node line (F + k)² = F/4, where the uniform reacting state appears.

Alan Turing proposed in 1952 that two diffusing chemicals could break a uniform tissue into spots and stripes. John Pearson's 1993 Science paper "Complex Patterns in a Simple System" mapped this Gray-Scott model and sorted its patterns into Greek-letter classes, plotted faintly on the map. Space pause, R seed, C clear, S sweep, [ ] brush.
